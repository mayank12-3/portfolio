04Case studyServify · 2024–2026

Strangling a monolith in production

Four enterprise partners, four regions, zero acceptable downtime, and a roadmap nobody would let us pause.

01 — The situation

One codebase served Apple, Samsung, HP and AT&T across India, MEA, EU and the US. Every release was a coordinated event, and a bug in one partner's warranty flow could take down another partner's claims flow. The incident rate reflected exactly that.

02 — The real risk

Not the architecture — the team. Ten engineers across four squads, each with their own partner commitments, could just as easily have built four inconsistent services and landed on a distributed monolith. That is worse than what we started with.

03 — What I did

Refused the big-bang rewrite. Drew service boundaries around business capability, standardised API contracts before anyone wrote a service, then strangled the monolith one capability at a time — old path live until the new one proved itself. Observability, distributed logging and Jenkins pipelines went in ahead of the migration, so a deploy became boring rather than an event.

Four squads came out of it shipping to one standard rather than four. That outcome matters more to me than the incident graph.

Product 01 — headless CMS

Itinerary CMS

A self-hosted headless CMS and marketing frontend for safari itineraries. Editors stack blocks into pages and multi-day trips, and the same content renders as a web page or a print-ready PDF.

  • Block-based page builder. Every section is a Payload block wired to the frontend through one render map — new layouts without a deploy.
  • Itinerary engine. Day-by-day timeline, camps and lodges, expected sightings, inclusions, gallery, reviews and FAQ.
  • Luxury PDF export. A PDF-only render mode with its own cover and page-break rules — one content source, two very different outputs.
  • Attributed enquiries. A shared form context records which page and which block opened every lead.

Product 02 — multi-tenant SaaS

Safari Booking Management

The operations platform underneath. Each operator signs in on their own subdomain with their own branding, users and roles; the Halisi console runs on the apex domain and onboards new tenants.

  • Tenant isolation by subdomain. Middleware resolves host to tenant; every query, role and asset is scoped to it — one deployment serving the console and every operator.
  • Money, carefully. Bookings into verification, supplier ledger, credit notes and multi-currency instalments — cash-based P&L bucketed by payment date, not sold price.
  • Operations views. Cashflow heatmap of inflow against unpaid supplier balance, a bookings calendar, and analytics that merge multi-country trips into one destination line.
  • Auth for shared laptops. Session JWT plus a device cookie, email OTP on every new device, invite and set-password flows, per-tenant role validation.
